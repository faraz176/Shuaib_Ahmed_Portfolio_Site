Clinic Cabling and Switch Installation

Routing Ethernet through the workspace, tracing existing cabling, reinstalling an Aruba switch, and leaving an explicit handoff for unfinished work.

Context

Physical-layer work across an active office buildout.

This visit supported a clinic office installation involving workstation setup, Ethernet routing, and preparation for additional network equipment. Customer names, site addresses, ticket identifiers, personnel details, and reporting fields are intentionally omitted.

Work performed

Turn the cut-sheet into an orderly physical installation.

  • Installed office computers and routed Ethernet cabling through drywall to the intended port for a planned time-clock installation.
  • Coordinated data-jack placement, traced cables, and followed the supplied cut-sheet for switch-port connections.
  • Reinstalled an Aruba switch and adjusted rack positioning to improve cable management.
  • Performed racking and stacking and prepared the installation area for future AP and UPS work.

Closeout discipline

Separate completed work from the remaining checklist.

The original closeout says the majority of the major tasks were completed, not all of them. It lists printer configuration and basic cable testing, time-clock installation, and AP installation as remaining work. This report therefore does not claim that a printer fault was fixed, full network validation was completed, or the additional devices were placed into service.

Operational takeaway

A useful handoff preserves uncertainty.

Field documentation should identify what physically changed, what was actually tested, and what still requires ownership. Keeping those categories separate prevents a successful cable run or a reseated switch from being mistaken for end-to-end service validation.
